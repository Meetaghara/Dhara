## 01 · MLense — No-code AutoML product launch

**Summary line:** Taking a no-code AutoML platform from proof of concept to production launch on Google Cloud in 12 weeks.

**Meta**
- Role: AI Product Lead, Data Pixel Ltd
- Timeline: Nov 2024 – Nov 2025
- Team: 2 developers + a 5-person marketing agency
- Status: Launched — v1.0 on Google Cloud

**Overview**
- **The problem:** Model building was slow — a problem I saw first-hand. MLense set out to let business analysts and domain experts train and explain models in the browser, without coding.
- **The approach:** I built the roadmap around that problem, shipped a working MVP in month one, then ran six two-week sprints, each with agreed scope and acceptance criteria.
- **The impact:** MLense went from proof of concept (v0.1) to production launch on Google Cloud (v1.0) in 12 weeks, with 70 automated tests passing and 6 security issues fixed.

**Diagram — "12-week delivery"**
Six equal bars labelled Sprint 1 to Sprint 6 (two weeks each). Only these markers:
- At the start: "v0.1 · Proof of concept"
- Under Sprints 1–2: "Working MVP in month one"
- At the end: "v1.0 · Production launch on Google Cloud"
No per-sprint descriptions, no intermediate version numbers, no "acceptance met" badges.

**Section — Context**
Data Pixel Ltd was a UK start-up building no-code AI tools for business users. I owned its product, MLense: a no-code AutoML platform covering 7 machine-learning task types, built for business analysts and domain experts who don't code. I led 2 developers and a 5-person marketing agency.

**Section — How we delivered**
We ran delivery in 6 two-week sprints over 12 weeks, each with agreed scope and acceptance criteria. I ran client demos and logged every change request in writing, from new features to UX fixes.

**Section — What set MLense apart**
- Gemini-powered recommendations that pre-build a model pipeline from an uploaded dataset.
- An assistant that explains results in plain English.

**Section — Built to be self-serve**
- 4 pipeline templates, from beginner to expert.
- An in-platform documentation portal.
- An in-app feedback centre for bug reports and feature requests.
- An admin dashboard to review them.

**Section — Quality and go-to-market**
MLense launched with 70 automated tests passing and 6 security issues fixed.

I defined 3 target segments, led by business analysts and domain experts without coding skills, and wrote a 5-phase go-to-market plan, from LinkedIn content to performance partners. After a free launch, Pro and Team pricing was planned at US$29 and US$99 a month.

## 02 · Amazon — Improving how teams share resolved issues

**Summary line:** A proposal to get resolved issues into the guidance teams work from, faster.

**Meta**
- Role: Senior ML Data Associate II, Amazon (Cambridge, UK)
- Timeline: Nov 2025 – Present
- Team: Proposal author · impact mapped for 4 teams
- Status: Proposal under review

**Overview**
- **The problem:** Resolved SIM (issue-resolution) tickets took too long to reach the convention pages teams work from.
- **The approach:** I proposed linking each SIM to its task on the convention page, so every team works from one current source of guidance.
- **The impact:** The proposal is under review. Estimated gain: 80–85% in how well guidance is shared.

**Diagram — "How the proposal works" (conceptual)**

**Section — Context**
I annotate and quality-check about 5,000 multimodal training data points a month — audio, video, image and text — and resolve edge cases that fall outside the annotation guidelines. I noticed that resolved SIM tickets took too long to reach the convention pages teams work from.

**Section — The proposal**
Link each SIM to its task on the convention page, so every team works from one current source of guidance.

**Section — The change plan**
- Impact and benefits mapped for 4 teams.
- A 6-step implementation plan.
- A pilot on 1 live workflow before full rollout.

**Section — A feedback loop for managers**
With SIMs visible during L1 audits, tasks that cause repeated overturns show up early and can be fixed for the whole team in a huddle.

## 03 · MSc Research — Multimodal content prediction

**Summary line:** Predicting YouTube view counts from metadata and LLM-scored transcripts.

**Meta**
- Role: MSc dissertation
- Timeline: Sep 2023 – Sep 2024
- Team: Queen Mary University of London
- Status: Completed · MSc Big Data Science, Distinction

**Overview**
- **The question:** Could LLM-scored transcripts predict a YouTube video's view count better than metadata alone?
- **The approach:** I hand-collected 500 videos and designed an 11-question LLM scoring framework that turned transcripts into model features, then compared 3 approaches.
- **The result:** The LLM-scored model beat both the metadata-only baseline and a visual-only deep learning model, with 79% lower average error than the metadata-only baseline.

**Chart — "Average error, relative to the metadata-only baseline"**
- Metadata-only baseline: 100
- LLM-scored model: 21 (79% lower)
Caption under the chart: "The visual-only deep learning model was also outperformed; it isn't plotted here."

**Section — The dissertation**
*Multimodal AI for Predicting the Success of Content Creation* — MSc Big Data Science, Queen Mary University of London, awarded with Distinction.

**Section — An 11-question LLM scoring framework**
I designed an 11-question framework — covering clarity, engagement, credibility and more — that used an LLM to score each transcript. The scores became features for the prediction model.

**Section — Three approaches compared**
- A metadata-only baseline.
- A visual-only deep learning model.
- A model using metadata plus LLM-scored transcripts.

**Section — Result**
The LLM-scored model beat both the metadata-only baseline and the visual-only model, with 79% lower average error than the metadata-only baseline.

**Key numbers**
| Value | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 79% | Lower average error | Vs the metadata-only baseline |
| 500 | Videos | Hand-collected |
| 11 | Scoring questions | Clarity, engagement, credibility and more |
| 3 | Approaches compared | Metadata, visual, LLM-scored |
